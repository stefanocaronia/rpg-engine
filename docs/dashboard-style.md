# The dashboard's style

How the player's dashboard looks, moves and speaks: the tokens and parts of its style sheet, [src/dashboard/style/](../src/dashboard/style/index.css), and when to use each. Use what is here before adding anything; a new part is described here when it is added. Where the page does not follow this guide yet, the guide wins: the list at the end says what to bring in line.

The dashboard is the table beside the chat where the game is played. It reads like a video game's interface, not an office app: clear first, then good to look at, then a little delight. It is generic: it never names a game's concepts; a game system brings its words and its layout as data.

## 1. Tokens

Every colour, corner, shadow and duration is a custom property of `:root`. A raw value outside `:root` is a bug, except inside the own palettes of the things drawn: the maps, the dice, the tokens and coins, the robot; white and black only as light and shade, a little of them over a colour. A paler or darker tone of a token is `color-mix()` of it.

### Colour

Two seeds, the robot's navy and gold. Each token is `light-dark()`, so both themes come from one rule; `light-dark()` takes colours only, never a shadow or a length.

| Token | Use |
|---|---|
| `--paper` | The page's ground |
| `--panel` | A card |
| `--panel-2` | A tone above the card: something nested, or the dark theme's raised parts |
| `--float` | Controls that stand on a card: buttons, fields, segmented controls |
| `--cell` | The label's cell in a card's lines |
| `--rule` | Borders and dividing lines |
| `--ink` | Text |
| `--muted` | Secondary text, inactive tabs, icons of quiet controls |
| `--accent` | What can be pressed and what is chosen: links, the focus ring, the chosen tab, the cards' icons, a page's head. Navy on the light page, gold on the dark one |
| `--gold`, `--on-gold` | The robot's gold, the same in both themes: the one main action of a view, a chosen value, the player's turn, a main quest, the highest roll. Text on it is `--on-gold` |
| `--gold-ink` | Gold as text or a line, readable on the light page |
| `--ally` | The party's side, a success, something gained |
| `--low`, `--on-low` | Danger: a problem, a failure, a foe the master marked, death; text on it |
| `--warn` | A warning, as the game being away. On the dark page it is the gold: never beside gold that means "chosen" |
| `--disc` | The dark disc under a token, in both themes |
| `--veil`, `--on-veil`, `--on-veil-muted`, `--veil-low` | Over a picture, the same in both themes: its dark veil, the words on it, a deadline near; on the veil `--accent` is `--gold`, for its links and the focus ring |
| `--cover`, `--cover-deep`, `--cover-main`, `--cover-main-deep` | A quest's cover without a picture, a main quest's in gold |
| `--m-*`, `--l-*`, `--bio-*`, `--doc-*` | The paper of the maps and of documents, and their inks, the same in both themes (see Paper) |
| `--season-*` | The four seasons, in the calendar alone and always beside their word |

- A tinted ground is `color-mix(in srgb, var(--tone) 10%, var(--panel))` with a border at 45% toward `--rule`, as a notice; a chosen chip is the accent at 14%.
- Colour never says a thing alone: a shape, an icon or a word goes with it (the foe's ring and the word, the failure's square and its colour).
- Text 4.5:1 and controls 3:1 against their ground, in both themes. Check the light theme too: gold on cream is the usual failure.

### Type

The system's sans for everything (`body`: 15px/1.4, `--font`). The serif (`--serif`) is the story's voice only: the campaign's name, the master's question and words in the chat, what is happening now in a scene, the names on the maps, and the text of documents found in the world.

| Size | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `0.72rem`, uppercase, `letter-spacing: 0.08em` | 700 | An eyebrow: the small label over a title or a group (`.eyebrow`), muted; `.eyebrow.accent` when it says "now" |
| `0.8rem` | 400–650 | Small: counts, tooltips, footnotes |
| `0.85rem` | 400–650 | Secondary: a row's meta, a closed card's preview, chips |
| body (inherit, 15px) | 400 | Text |
| `1.125rem` | 700 | A card's title (`h3`) |
| `1.25rem` | 700 | A title over a picture or in a dialog: a banner, the master's question, a quest's cover |
| `1.4rem` | 700 | A page's title (`h2` in `PageHead`) |
| `1.6rem` and up | 700, 800 for the player's two moments | One number that is the point of its box: a total, a modifier; at 800 the roll's total, the death save's word and a package card's outcome |

Weights: 400 text, 600 a word that stands out in a line (a chip, a filter, a notice, a name in a row) and the serif titles, 650 controls (buttons, tabs), 700 titles, names and numbers. The maps draw their labels in px, in the map's own units. Numbers that line up are `.num` (tabular figures). Items of a list start with a capital; a field's abbreviation (AC, WIS) is all capitals.

### Room, corners, depth

- Room in steps of 2 px up to 12, then 16 (`--gap`, between cards and stacks and around the page) and 24. A card's padding is 16 px inline; its header is at least 52 px tall.
- Corners: `--radius-xs` 4 (tooltips), `--radius-s` 6 (list chips, controls inside controls), `--radius-m` 8 (buttons, fields, icon buttons), `--radius-l` 12 (cards, notices), `--radius-xl` 16 (banners, dialogs). Round (`50%`) only tokens and dots. A pill (`999px`) only for a choice among peers (filter chips, verbs, the maps' tabs) and a switch's track; never a button or a card. A thin bar has `--radius-xs`, its ends round; a mark that is square to tell it from a round one, `calc(var(--radius-xs) / 2)`.
- Depth: on the light page a card has `--lift, --raise`; what floats over the page (dialogs, sheets, the dice's button, tonal buttons) has `--overlay`; the phone's bar at the window's foot `--raise-up`; a little drawn edge `--shade`. On the dark page depth is the surface's tone; only what floats casts a shadow. The page's light from above is `--glow`.

### Motion

| Token | Use |
|---|---|
| `--quick` 100 ms | State layers, tooltips, a press |
| `--short` 250 ms | Most transitions: a card opening, a colour changing, a tab |
| `--medium` 350 ms | A panel or sheet coming in |
| `--flight` 450 ms | Something that travels: the result plate flying to its row |
| `--long` 600 ms, `--linger` 900 ms | A mark that fades after it is seen |
| `--wait` 500 ms | The moment a pointer rests on a control before its tooltip shows |

`--ease` for most, `--ease-out` for what comes in, `--ease-in` for what leaves, `--ease-bounce` for one pop. A loop (breathing, pulsing) only for what waits for the player: their turn, a check asked, the robot; its period, in seconds, is its own, as is how long a fresh mark stays. A short step between things that come one after the other is a share of `--quick`. Motion never makes the player wait to act; script that times a motion of its own uses the same token's length.

Motion is added inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, so that with reduced motion the state is simply there; script checks `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)")` before a smooth scroll or an animation. Only what leaves keeps its animation with reduced motion, run at once (`animation-duration: 0.01s`), so that it reaches its end.

## 2. Parts

**Page.** A section is a `PageHead` (its icon and title in the accent) over a `.page-stack` of cards, with room at the bottom for the dice's button. The page never changes section by itself. A section too long for one stack follows the scheme of Packages and Settings: its head, a line that says what a change there does, its tabs, then the chosen tab's cards, always open, each with its icon and title (`CardHead`) and a few rows on one matter. A section made after them reuses these parts, and adds one of its own only where they do not fit.

**Tabs.** Parts of one section are tabs under its head (`PageTabs`, `.page-tabs`): words in `--muted`, the chosen one in the accent with a 3 px bar under it, 40 px tall (44 on touch). On a phone they close up, and when they still do not fit the row scrolls sideways; it never wraps and never widens the page. The tab is in the address (`#/world/lore`), changed with `location.replace`; an item opened inside it is too (`#/world/lore/Bertoldo`), pushed, so that Back returns to it and the master can give a link. Arrows move along the tabs, Home and End go to the ends, and one Tab stop reaches the row. A tab may carry a mark after its name (`.tab-mark`): a count in the accent, or what is missing in `--low`, with its tip and in the tab's name for a screen reader.

**Card.** `<details class="group">` with `CardHeader`: its icon (20 px, stroked in the accent), its title, a preview while closed, the chevron. It stays as the player left it (`useOpen`); on a phone cards start closed but the first. A card that is always open is a `<section class="card">` with `CardHead` and its `.card-body`. No card inside a card: inside one, sections under small headings.

**Buttons.** A button of words is `.button`, 38 px tall (44 on touch): `.gold` for the one main action of a view, `.quiet` (accent words, no border) for a secondary one, `.danger` (red edge) for one that loses something, full red at its last step. A button of an icon is `.icon-button` (40 px), always with `aria-label` and the same words in `data-tip`; `.tonal` (48 px, raised) when it floats over the page. What goes somewhere is a link (`<a href>`); what does something is a button.

**Chips.** A thing in a list is a chip (`.chips li`: the accent at 11%, `--radius-s`). An effect on someone is a chip (`.effect`); one the game draws (`condition-icons`) has its icon inside, 15 px, before its word, and where few fit, as the party's strip, the icon alone in its square chip with its word in the tooltip and as its name (`.effect.icon-only`). A filter is a row of pill chips (`.filter-chips`, `aria-pressed`, a muted count after the name): one Tab stop, arrows, Home and End along it. A search is a field with its magnifier inside (`.search`) that filters as it is typed. Two to five values of one setting are a `.segmented` control; a setting whose parts each take a level, as a custom difficulty, shows them under its row, set in by a rule on the start side, each with its name, what it is and its own control (`.setting-parts`), never as rows of their own. A setting that counts for nothing while another holds it, as the prose while an author is written or what goes into the repository while commits are off, stays to choose: `.setting.off`, its chosen value's gold paled and its meaning muted, never by opacity, with a line in italics under it that says why, read by a screen reader with the control. The game's time and day belong to its world: in the row under the sections, beside where the party is (`.top-place`), which stays as the top draws itself together down the page, what sticks under the top sticking under it (`--top-space`); at its end, each its icon in the accent and its words, never chips; on a phone, which has no such row, the time alone under the campaign's name. At that row's end, where the page has its column beside it (from 900 px), its one switch: an icon in a `.segmented` of one, as the chat's places, pressed in gold while the column is shown, one name for a screen reader and its tip; closed, nothing stands at the window's edge and the page takes the whole width, as the browser remembers.

**Rows.** A list of things to open is a column of rows, each one button the row's width: the name in bold, its meta in `--muted` at 0.85rem, a number or distance at the end in `.num`. Sentences are lines, not chips. On the sheet a number the system rolls better or worse has an arrow beside its die, up in `--ally`, down in `--low`, both muted when they cancel out, and its tooltip says why; a load past a stage has the stage's word in `--warn` before it; a thing in a list of bonds has two rings after its name, and the list how many it holds of its most.

**Changed pages.** A page of the game system that the campaign changed has a small asterisk after its name wherever the page shows it, as a thing carried, a spell in a list or an option to choose (`ChangedMark`, `.changed-mark`): a note's mark, as in a book, never a pencil, which is the button that edits; six arms, never five, which read as a star, raised over the line, 0.75rem, in `--muted`, never the accent, which on the dark page is the gold of what is equipped or chosen. It is no control: its words are its name for a screen reader and its tooltip, and it stands after a name that acts, never inside it. In the head of a page read whole, as a sheet's band or a reader, the asterisk and its words are a line, the note itself, that links to the list (`ChangedLine`); on a phone, where no tooltip shows, that line says what the mark means. The list stands in Packages, in Installed, under the game system the campaign plays, and only while there is something to list, under a line that says how a line of it reads: each page by its name with Put back…, a line for each field, its label, what the system gave, an arrow, what the campaign has in bold, and the parts of its text rewritten by name; one changed only in what the character does not know, as a creature never met, by its name and a line that says so, never what changed nor how much. Put back… asks first, with the same lines turned round, what the campaign has, an arrow, what the system gives again in bold, or that the character does not know what goes, and that the page cannot undo it. While a scene is being played nothing is put back: a line in the ink says so over the list, the buttons are dimmed (`aria-disabled`, described by that line), and one pressed says it again in its row. What happened to a page is said in its row, and stays in its place once the page is back and its row gone. On a phone Put back… goes under the name when the two do not fit.

**List and reader.** Many things of a kind to browse and read, as what the character knows or the documents found: a list (a search field, filter chips, the rows grouped under eyebrows) and the open one in a reader beside it from 900 px; under that the reader takes the list's place, with a Back link at its top. Each row is a link to its item, named by its name and described by its meta (`aria-describedby`); the open one is `aria-current`, lit with a bar of the accent at its start. The open item is in the address; opening it moves the focus to the reader's title, Back returns it to its row. On a wide screen with nothing open the reader shows the list's first. An empty search says so in a line, with the way out.

**Extensions' widgets.** An extension puts its widgets in places the page keeps: a chip in the row of the world after the time and day, drawn as they are, its icon and words with no box, or, where its package says `where: top`, at the top's end beside the campaign's name (`.status-chip.widget-chip`), its icon and few words; where a row is short, the chips that do not fit are left unseen, what it says whole in its tip and for a screen reader, a link to its extension's card when one stands in a page, hidden on a phone; cards (`.widget-card`, a `.card` with `CardHead`) in the table's column over the rolls, or at the end of a page, after the page's own. A card draws only the page's parts: one thing large (`.widget-lead`: its icon at 40 px, its words at 1.25rem, its value at 1.6rem), text, chips, the boxes of `.line`, pips or a bar (good pips circles in `--ally`, bad ones squares in `--low`, by shape as well as colour; a progress as tall steps; after the lit ones, held ones half full, a failed one crossed, those the roll may light dashed), `.rows`, a table, a picture. The extension's icons are paths stroked in the accent as the page's; its words come translated, its numbers as values the page writes in its language, isolated right to left. A chip that does not fit the top's row is marked `.wrapped` and unseen, so that the row never clips its tooltips. Settings lists every widget with its place, but one made for each of the party or for a card of a question. That one names the state of whom it shows on as the page's own states are: its icon in their token's pip and in the turns' icons, tinted by its lead's tone (good `--ally`, bad `--low`, none grey as a fall), its word in their tip and name, never twice beside an effect of the same name; without it, one of the party at 0 is down, as a foe. It draws as a panel (`.person-panel`), as the 5e's death saves: under the name and in place of the card at hand, its good counts on the start side as circles, its bad ones on the end side as squares, each its label over its marks, its lead's word between them, at 1.25rem without a figure, with its package's figure over it as a band 34 px tall, the panel tinted by the lead's tone (quiet without one, grey with the skull when the widget says dead); with one side only, the word beside it, the two in the middle. In a row of the turns the same in one line, small, the figure 34 by 14, the icon of the state its word says hidden from a screen reader; in the party's strip its word and marks in place of the bar. A widget on the party that says no state, or that the campaign moved there, is a small card of its package among the boxes of values under the name and in the card at hand (`.line-value.person-box`): each count, lead and value a box as the system's own, its label over its pips, words or number. The counters they count leave the line of values.

**A package's drawings.** A package (an extension or a game system) may draw with its own page code inside places the page keeps: a figure in place of a count or of a lead, in a card of a roll, a widget's card or a chip of the top; the whole inside of a card of a roll, the page keeping its question, its Close and the field for the player's words; a card at the end of a page (Scene, sheet, World, Journal), after the page's own cards and the widgets', in the page's frame with its title and icon; or a page of its own at `#/pkg/<package>/<name>`, under the page's head with its title, linked by its title from the package's row in Packages. The package says where the navigation has its page: a tab after the sections, as they are drawn, with its icon and title cut short past 12em (in the bar at the bottom of a phone, a fifth of it); or, as one that says nothing, in the packages' icon at the row's end, which then opens a menu of those pages over Packages (on a phone, in the page's menu under Packages, set in), lit while one of them is shown. Opened from the navigation, the focus goes to its title; loaded by its address, it stays at the page's start. Each place is a shadow root that takes this style sheet, so the page's classes and tokens hold inside it and its own rules stay there; the page's Preact draws it, with the kit `rpg-engine/page` (`Button`, `IconButton`, `Icon`, `Dice`, `Chips`, `Portrait`, `Who`, `Track`, `Text`, `Value`, `Choices`), and its own style sheet after this one, when its module gives one, which wins over the page's layers whatever its selectors, but never over the focus ring and the tooltips. A figure keeps the room its package declares while its module comes, at most the width of its place, its shape kept; the page turns it right to left when it is declared mirrored. The count it draws keeps its name and value in words for a screen reader, and its count in figures under it; the lead it draws keeps its words and value, the figure in place of its icon: beside it in a page's card, in one row, under it where the card is narrow, as in the table's column; in a chip, the figure of the widget's lead takes the icon's place. What breaks says, in its place, the error and whose code it is.

**Strips.** A compact summary that leads to its whole view, as the party's tokens or a followed quest: the whole strip is one link, lit as one.

**Notices.** The page's own messages, over the page under its top (`.notice`): `.problem` in `--low`, the game away in `--warn`, a word to know in the accent, each with its icon. One with somewhere to act has its link after its words, and a cross at its end when it may be hidden, as a newer RPG Engine's, hidden until the next version. The master's words never go in a notice.

**The master's question.** What the master asks stays over the page, in sight while it scrolls, with the robot and the question in the serif. Where the campaign's table keeps the chat and the chat has the question, a check, a choice, a toss or one answered in words is asked in the chat, in sight, and not here; the dice of a rest, a package's card and a square of the map stay here, without the field of the player's own words while the chat takes them. With more than one player character, the portrait of the one it asks takes the robot's place, the robot small on its edge, and their name is said over the title ("For Mira") and before the folded question. A card that belongs to a question (a rest's dice, a package's question, as the 5e's death save) stands in its frame, edged in the accent; while one is there, the notice of the player's turn waits. A rest's dice with no package's card are the question's own buttons, one for each counter of dice with what is left, and the one that ends it. Nothing else uses that frame. A package's card (`.ask-card`) lays its widget on a stage: who acts on the left (`.who-acts`, their coin at 64 px, their name over what they do), the middle count large, the other counts one over the other on the right, each its label before its marks, the facts under them; on a phone who acts over the middle, the others in a row under it. No colour of the moment, unless its package gives one (`tint`): then the frame is of that token, deeper at the edges, without the robot, as the 5e's death save in `--low` while dying, `--ally` stable, `--gold` back on their feet, `--muted` dead; the lock's card has none. Under the stage the roll's button or the choices a die asks for, over the rule; once the dice have spoken, the widget's lead with its tone where the buttons were, at 1.6rem and 800. It keeps its place through the questions of one attempt, its marks moving once the dice land; on a phone it stays in sight while the player rolls and scrolls with the page once told. While it is there, the same widget beside the page hides.

**Dice and coins.** The tray is a column of dice over its button, the d20 nearest to it, and above them the coin: one to three coins, never thrown with dice, nothing added to them nor doubled. A toss shown, the player's or the master's, lands in the tray on its faces as a check's dice do; the master's dice only when the master says so, else they stay in the rolls. The master throws with a set of their own, drawn by the campaign's name among those that are not the player's, for anyone outside the party; the party rolls with the player's. A toss the master asks has the coins on its button, and the tray's coins, as many, answer it. The coin bears the design the player chooses in Settings, under its material: "Sun and moon" at first, or crown and tower, rose and skull, planet and comet, gear and lightning, eye and star, heads first, each a mesh of make-coin.py loaded only when a coin of it is thrown; the master's coins bear the player's design. Its material is what the player chooses beside the dice's set: by default the dice's own material, its body the coin's and its ink the pictures', grain included, or one of five metals, gold, silver, copper, bronze and burnished iron, each with its dark patina, iron's pictures inlaid in silver. A clear set's coin shows only its nearest face, the page through it, never its other face's picture. The master's coins are of the master's dice. Its pictures (`CoinFace`, drawn from `coin-pictures.ts`, which make-coin.py writes, in the look given or the player's) show its faces as they are, heads' picture in the ink on the body, tails' in the body on the ink, and its words, heads or tails, stand beside them, on the plate in place of the total and in the rolls on the line of the dice. A coin or a design chosen in Settings is tossed once to be seen, as a set's d20 rolls; in Settings each design shows its heads over its tails, and a value's long name wraps beside its picture. On the plate the coins keep their width, their words wrapping beside them.

**Choices waiting.** What the master prepares for one of the party to choose when the player likes, as a new level: a notice in their sheet's band (`.choices-notice`, the accent's tint, never the turn's gold; the ally's once confirmed), a chevron on their token in the party's strip and a dot on the Party tab, their words in the links' names, nothing that loops. Their page, `#/choices/<who>`, lights Party: the steps as chips, then each choice with its options as cards in a grid, a ring for one among them and a square for several, gold once chosen as the setup's, dashed when closed (full, a roll made, confirmed); a long list has its search with the ones chosen over it as chips. An option that names a page has Read, which opens it as a list's reader (`#/choices/<who>/<page>`) with Choose inside. An option that rolls throws the tray's dice, its total at 1.6rem and 800 once they land, or takes the player's own dice in a field. What is still to choose and the gold Confirm end the page: no bar fixed over it, no dialog.

**The master's chat.** Where the campaign's table keeps the chat with the master, the page has it in a section of its own, Chat, whose tab stands right after the Party's, never last; served by a chat, the page shows no sign of it. Its tab has a dot, as the Party's for the choices that wait, while the master said something the player has not seen there. The chat is never a card among the page's: it stands on a ground of its own, restful, with no shadow, in the light theme the paper with a little gold, in the dark one a near-black night; in its section that ground keeps the box's edge and corners. Open, it is as tall as the window leaves it from the page's top: the messages scroll inside it, and the player's words stay in sight under them. The player's words stand at the end side on a tone above the card, never the accent; the master's are text as they write it, a paragraph keeping its line breaks, across the chat's whole width, with no bubble, in the serif at 1.0625rem, their words out of the game and the marks of rolls in the text's face; a word of the table's, as a turn that failed, is a notice in `--low` with its icon. The open chat, while it is still, offers a button in place of writing, which sends the master a sentence as the player's own words out of the game; it goes as a turn begins, and who would rather write, writes. A chat taken up, which the master remembers, or one cut as the game was closed in the middle of the story, ends with a word of the table's that is no trouble, on a quiet ground with its icon in `--accent`: what it is, and a plain button, to take up the story or to pick up from there; with no message kept, that word stands alone at the chat's foot. A new chat, with nothing said, has an invitation in the middle of its empty room, not a notice: that the master is ready, in the serif, over a gold button, Begin. A chat the table takes up by itself is there as the page comes: where one is opened never shows first. Words out of the game, the master's `*[OOC: …]*` and the player's brackets, marked or plain, stand apart from the story's by their shape, never by a colour of their own: smaller, dimmed to `--muted` and no further, in italics, in a dashed edge, without their brackets and their mark, which a screen reader hears said before them, «Out of the game». Out of the game the table talks as people do in a chat app: a paragraph of the master's that is all of them is a dashed bubble at the start, a message of the player's that is all of them a dashed bubble at the end, each on a faint ground (`--ink` at 5%) with its tail at its foot on its own side, dashed too and open where it meets the bubble, mirrored by the bubble's own way of writing; some among the story's are a box in the line, with no tail; a whole block has its mark on the dashed edge at its start corner, as a fieldset's legend, the letters OOC in small capitals, upright, in `--muted` on the grounds it lies on, their meaning in a tooltip, and the words among the story's have none; they are so from their first word, while the master still writes them. Never the brackets of a link, of a page's name or of code, and the master's plain brackets are the story's. The messages scroll with the wheel, a finger and the keys, with no bar in sight: a shade of the card's ground at the list's top or foot says there is more that way, never words. The list follows what comes only while its end is in sight: farther up it stays, and a pill in the middle of its foot, an arrow down and «Go to now», leads back to the end, at once, never smoothly, with a dot of the accent on its corner when something came there meanwhile. A roll stands in the story by the engine's mark, which the master writes where the roll decides, `[roll <id>]`: there the page draws the roll as the engine told it, a pill in the line on a tone above the card, with its die, what it is and its total in `--accent`, the dice one by one in its tooltip; no token, which is its line's, and no colour of success or failure. Bold or italics around a mark alone are left out; a mark of a roll the chat does not have draws nothing, never its own letters, nor does one still being written. A clock's mark, `[clock <page>]`, is the same pill with the clock's name for the player and how full it is, as `2/4`, in `--accent`; the mark of the turns, `[turns]`, the same pill with the round and who acts in order, whoever acts now in `--accent`. Both draw what the message kept once complete: a mark with no data draws nothing, while the message is written too. Anything else the master puts in brackets is the story's, as written. While the master answers, a line under the messages says so in the player's words, never a command's name: the master is answering, is rolling the dice, or is waiting for the player's answer. ## 3. Words

- To the player, in the second person: "your character", "the master". - What the player does outside the page, as starting a new chat after an update, is a numbered list of short steps in their words, framed in the accent where it was asked, never a notice that goes away.

## 5. Drawing inside the page

Rules for a package's page code, which the page does not check:

1. Colours, corners, lengths of time and curves only from the tokens; no colour of its own, no font of its own.
2. No words inside a figure: words and numbers are the page's parts (`Text`, `Value`, a track's label), which translate, isolate right to left and are read aloud. A figure without words can be mirrored.
3. A figure is `aria-hidden`: what it means is in its part, a count's label and value or a lead's words. A whole inside of a card says what it shows in words too.
4. Every motion inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, or only when `reducedMotion` is false, with the tokens' lengths; it moves from `before` to the data given, which a card of a roll gives once the dice land; it never makes the player wait, and its buttons can be pressed while it moves.
5. It draws only inside its place: no `document`, no globals, no style in the HTML (`style=""` and `<style>` break the page's security policy; style objects, its module's `style` and style sheets linked from its folder do not); no address outside the dashboard; pictures from its own folder.
6. Its room, declared, fits at 360 and at 1280 px; its buttons are the kit's, reached by the keyboard, with their names.
7. Seen in both themes, right to left, with reduced motion, at 360 and 1280 px, before it ships.

## 6. The style sheet's files

Plain CSS in cascade layers, no preprocessor. `index.css` names the layers in their order and imports the files; the build joins them, in that order, into the one `style.css` that the page and the shadow roots of packages' drawings read (`join.mjs`: a constructed sheet takes no `@import`).

| Layer | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `guarantees` | What no style sheet may take away: every `outline` (the focus ring, and the rings drawn as outlines), no `clip-path` or `mask` on a control, and the tooltips, all `!important` | `guarantees.css` |
| `tokens` | The custom properties of `:root`, and the theme | `tokens.css` |
| `theme` | The game's style: the theme of the campaign's game system, custom properties alone, which the engine writes and the page adopts while the player keeps it on | `/api/theme` |
| `base` | The elements before any part: the box, the window, the body, the titles | `base.css` |
| `parts` | The parts of section 2, a file each | `parts/` |
| `pages` | What belongs to one page | `pages/` |
| `theme-style` | A theme's own style sheet, its rules over the page's, which the page adds while the player keeps it on | the theme's folder |

A later layer wins over an earlier one whatever the selectors; inside a layer, specificity then order, as always. A style sheet without a layer, as a package's own, wins over every layer but the guarantees: they are `!important` and the first layer, and among `!important` declarations the first layer wins, so no sheet that declares layers of its own takes them away either.

### The game's style

A game system may name a theme ([game-systems.md](game-systems.md#theme)): colours, corners and faces, and the hooks below, light and dark. It changes values, never rules: the page's rules read them, each in its part's layer, with today's look as their default. Settings, in Dashboard, turns it off for that system in this browser ("Theme"); off, the page is as without it.

| Hook | What it changes | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `--font`, `--title-font`, `--serif` | The text's face, the titles' (a card's title, 1.125rem, and up, and the campaign's name), the story's voice | `body`, the titles, the serif's places |
| `--number-font` | The big numbers: a tile's value, a widget's lead, the round, a roll's total, the calendar's date, a card's outcome | those numbers |
| `--title-case`, `--title-variant`, `--title-spacing`, `--title-glow` | The titles' capitals or small capitals, their spacing, a glow drawn as their shadow | the titles |
| `--page-texture`, `--bar-texture` (and `-size`, `-repeat`) | A picture under the page and under the top, always under `--texture-veil`, the page's own colour at 62% | `body`, `.top` |
| `--bar-edge` | A line under the top | `.top` |
| `--card-edge` | The cards' edge | each card's border |
| `--card-inner-line` | A second line inside the cards, an outline 5 px in that takes no room | the cards |
| `--card-ornament`, `--card-ornament-mirrored` | A drawing in a card's lower corner at the end, 26 px, in the edge's colour, turned right to left, left out of a folded card, whose corner is its chevron's; `--card-ornament-layer` places it | each card's background |
| `--card-corner` | The cards' corners cut, notched or scooped (`corner-shape`, where the browser draws it, else round), as large as `--radius-l` | the cards and their headers |
| `--card-texture-layer` | A texture under the cards, drawn in the grain's colour under the card's own at 70%, its words measured where it is thickest; a folded card keeps it | each card's background |
| `--button-radius`, `--button-corner` | The shape of a button of words: rounded as the page's, square, a pill or cut | `.button`, `.segmented`, the master's question, a sheet's go and cancel, the turn's notice |
| `--tab-radius`, `--tab-corner`, `--tab-chosen`, `--tab-bar` | The tab chosen on a tint of the accent, in a shape, instead of the bar under it | `.sections a`, `.page-tabs button`, the phone's bar |
| `--head-picture` (and `-size`, `-repeat`), `--head-picture-shown` | A picture at the end of the top, in the room the chips leave beside the campaign's name, to the window's edges, fading toward the name; under no words but the chips', which have their own ground; not on a phone | `.top::before` |
| `--lift`, `--raise`, `--raise-up`, `--overlay` | The shadows: soft as the page's, sharp, none or a glow, in a colour of the theme | everything that lifts |
| `--doc-paper`, `--doc-fold`, `--doc-rule`, `--m-land`, `--map-paper`, `--map-floor`, `--map-grid`, `--map-fog` | The paper of the documents and of the maps, the same in both modes, its folds, rules, floor, grid and fog drawn of it; the ink stays the page's | `.paper`, the world's map, a place's map |
| `--portrait-radius`, `--portrait-corner`, `--portrait-rim`, `--portrait-frame` | The portraits' shape, the glass's rim only on a round one, and a frame inside their edge over the picture, never in a foe's red | `.token` |
| `--dice-body`, `--dice-ink` | The set Theme's dice and coins | `dice/sets.ts` |

A card's title that the theme's face makes wider than its card, and the campaign's name, go back to the face and spacing they have without a theme (`.title-too-wide`), that title alone, as the page measures them. The page's icons of the sections and the top may be drawn anew by name, as lines the page draws in its own colour. What stays the page's: the layout, room and sizes, motion, the state layer, the focus ring and the tooltips, the robot, the player's dice sets, the ink of the maps and of documents, the words, right to left and the phone. A theme's own style sheet may change any of those but the focus ring and the tooltips: the page links a sheet of one line from the server that imports it into the last layer, `theme-style`, so that none of its braces leaves that layer and its `url()` come from the theme's folder; Settings lets the player turn it off for that theme ("Theme's own style"), say the page does not check it, and never show it themselves, so that the switch stays in reach. What is guaranteed is the ring's colour and shape and the tooltip, not all a sheet can do: one that clips, fades or filters what has the focus, or makes the page unreadable, the player turns off there, at `#/settings` if the page shows nothing else. Beside the theme, a game system's page module may give `pageStyle`, a style sheet over the whole page without a layer, with the same switch; its own risk when the page's classes change.

A new rule goes in the file of its part or page, inside that file's `@layer` block, what moves with it, inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` (motion has no layer of its own: it would win over the states that stop it); an `outline`, or anything of a tooltip, in `guarantees.css`, `!important`, its colours the `--guard-*` ones, never a token: they are set there on every element, `!important`, the theme's written there by the engine, and registered as colours in `index.css` outside every layer, so that no rule or `@property` of a theme's own style sheet changes them; the tooltip sets every property anew from `all: initial`, its pseudo-elements put out, and `tooltip.ts` places it with `top` and `left` inline. A new file is imported in `index.css` in its layer's place. Selectors stay as flat as they are; nesting comes into a file when it is rewritten. A rule kept in another layer than its file's says why in a comment over it.
