# Checklist

The same list after every run, on both sessions. Part A is read in `dialogue.md` (P1, M1, P2…); part B in `turns/M*.jsonl`, `campaign-after/` and its `log.jsonl`. Count from `turns/`, which holds every call of the master and of its subagents, whole results and the `permission_denials` of each turn.

For each item: **yes**, **no** or **n/a**, with the proof (message, turn, log id, file). An item with several parts is "no" if one fails: say which. "n/a" only when the situation never came up. In about 65 messages some will not come up (physical dice, a stop, an impossible action, a question on a hidden roll, a companion): that is not a failure, and they are tried again at the next phase.

Items marked *(computable)* can be counted from `turns/` and the files; `count-playtest.mjs` counts the numbers of B12 and parts of the others. The run script checks only B1.

A "no" on B1 voids the run: fix the setup and play again. A "no" on A1, A2 or B2 fails it. Part C has no verdict that fails the run.

The filled list goes to `private/project/playtest/<scenario>-<date>.md`, never here.

## A. The master's craft

1. **Behind the scenes** (D-70, D-73, D-78; core 1). No master message, in setup, in play or between `[ ]`, speaks of tools, commands, pages, files, folders, the log, the engine or extensions, or of a game system as something installed, not even when the player asks about the sheet, memory or saving; none shows a roll's id, or its fingerprint or proof unasked. "Campagna" and "sistema" as players say them at a table are fine: «la nostra campagna», «a che gioco giochiamo?», «che sistema usiamo?». Words to check: tool, comando, pagina, file, cartella, registro, log, salv-, annot-, motore, estensione, install-, impronta, rpg, the tool names.
2. **Secrets kept** (core 5). Every secret block of the starting pages, every hidden roll and every plan not yet out reaches the player only after the character finds it in the story; the message before shows how.
3. **The choice is the player's** (play 1). No master message makes the character act, speak, feel or decide what the player did not say; every description ends with the situation open to the player.
4. **Stakes before the roll, the player's go** (play 2; D-41, D-140). For every roll of the character out of a fight, the master says what is at stake either way and stops, and the roll comes after the player's go (or their dice's faces); the outcome keeps to the stakes. Every roll the player sees comes right after the moment it decides.
5. **Rolls only when they matter** (play 2). No roll for something certain or trivial: those get "yes" or "yes, but".
6. **Failure moves forward, no favoritism** (play 3, 9; core 4.4). Every failed roll brings a complication or a cost, and the story goes on; no failure hides a clue the investigation needs without another way to it. Earned consequences stand, a choice the master would not make goes unpunished, and enemies act by their intelligence and motives.
7. **Dangers signalled** (play 4). Before every harm to the character (wound, trap, ambush) an earlier message shows a sign of it, and every step of a clock (a `clock` change in the log) shows one before its event comes.
8. **The master rules and narrates** (core 4.5; play 7, 10; D-41, D-74). An outcome the player states («lo convinco») is treated as an attempt; an unusual but plausible idea gets a real chance (yes, yes-but or a roll), not the "right" way; an impossible action does not happen, said briefly within the story, with what is possible offered. The master narrates outcomes, reading the dice into the story, and at a fitting moment, such as the final blow on a major enemy, lets the player narrate (n/a if none came). A rule applied from memory is said to be from memory (in `[ ]` during play), without naming the engine or what is or is not installed.
9. **What is said is true** (core 4.1–4.3). No master message contradicts what was already said or revealed, in this session or an earlier one, or the pages as they were at that moment: names, places, who is where (as the containers list it, D-59), what happened. A fact not yet revealed may change, if the change is recorded with `write` at once (core 5.1, 5.3).
10. **NPCs and world as written** (core 3; play 12; D-76). NPCs speak and act as their pages say at that time (voice, wants, fears, what they know, say and believe, even wrongly) and give only what they know; one asked to come along answers by their motives; the plans on the pages advance with game time, and an event due on a day that passes happens, on screen or as news; a plan the story stops or changes has its events written in the same turn (`cancelled`, a later date, another `by`), and none of them comes due after that.
11. **Setup and session zero** (setup.md, session-zero.md; D-75, D-78, D-79). Setup is out of the game and without `[ ]`: it asks only for what is missing, at most three questions per message, the minimum to start first (D-83). It takes the language from how the player writes and confirms it, instead of asking. With a world already written it asks nothing about genre, tone or world, only what status lists (in `01-guadoscuro`, the language to confirm, difficulty, lethality, rigor, dice, units, companions, lines and veils, advancement), each with the values it takes. Session zero is play: the master builds the character and backstory with the player, offering choices and keeping theirs, before the adventure begins. Each mode is left with `mode` once the player confirms (D-77, D-221).
12. **The character's past in play** (play 8; session-zero.md). At least once a person, place, goal or bond from the backstory comes into the story.
13. **Pace** (play 5, 6; D-41). Play opens with something happening, not with "what do you do?"; session 2 opens with a short recap that matches session 1; scenes start late and end on a hook; travel and waiting are summed up; the story in each message is short and concrete, under 150 words outside [ ] unless more is truly needed (D-83, D-143). Session 2 starts at the game time where session 1 ended (D-72; core 10).
14. **Off-game and stop** (D-73, D-78; core 1.4, 6). During play, session zero included, the player's `[ ]` gets a short answer in `[ ]`, then the game goes on; the master's own off-game remarks in play are in `[ ]` too. Asked whether a hidden roll was real, the master gives its fingerprint in `[ ]`, with no tool talk. A stop signal drops that content at once, without asking why, and it does not come back.
15. **The player ends the session** (D-71; core 6). The master never ends the session or proposes to; no routine questions, in play or at the end; a question on how it went comes only after something did not work, about that thing, as multiple choice or a 1–10 score.
16. **The player's turn in a fight** (D-139, D-141; combat.md, play 13). The master tells the turns that are not the player's in order, each roll in brackets where it falls, and stops wherever the player can decide: their character's turn, rolls to stay alive included, a reaction they can take, a choice; a fall or a death is a moment of its own, told in the story. Fights are sized to the party as it stands, and no roll is set aside after it is made.

## B. Tools, log and files

1. **Isolated master** (D-48). `context.md` shows only the campaign's `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md` and only the `rpg` server; `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and `.claude/settings.json` are what `rpg init` writes at the clean commit in `meta.json`, and the campaign has nothing else in `.claude/`; `dist/` and the global `rpg` are that commit's build; Claude Code ran without `CLAUDE*` variables; every turn started with `rpg` connected, `dontAsk` and the planned model. *(checked by the run script: `end.json` does not say `isolation`)*
2. **Writes only through rpg** (D-46; core 1.3). No attempt, allowed or denied, to change a file other than through rpg: no Write, Edit or NotebookEdit, and no shell command that creates, changes, moves or deletes a file or runs a program other than `rpg`. `rpg` from a shell is the same command (D-46, D-62): not a fault here, whether permitted or denied; counted in B12. Every other call outside the rpg tools (a read-only shell command, Read outside the campaign, a subagent, a web search) is listed with its turn and judged one by one; its denials are counted in B12, not here. *(computable, subagents included)*
3. **Errors the master can fix**. Every error of an rpg tool is followed in the same turn by a call that succeeds; list each error message it could not act on. `init` is never called. *(computable, then read the list)*
4. **Settings** (D-8, D-141). `config` changes only real settings, to what the player chose or clearly implied; at the end of setup none is missing, each chosen by the player, or by the master when the player said so; no scene is refused for a mode not yet entered.
5. **Every roll logged** (core 2). Each roll the master narrates has its `roll` line, with `why`, and `who` when it is a character's.
6. **Physical dice** (the description of roll). When the player gives faces, the line has `physical: true` and exactly those faces.
7. **Hidden rolls** (D-6, D-73). Rolls the player must not see are `hidden`, with a fingerprint; a fingerprint shown to the player matches its line; a revealed roll has its `roll-revealed`.
8. **Facts in the files** (core 2, 3, 4.1; play 6; D-41, D-76). Every person, place, item or faction the master names for the first time has a page by the end of the scene; the player's character has a page (`role: pc`) with what session zero decided, backstory included; a character who joins the party has `role: companion`; nothing the master says it noted is missing; what the master promises about how they play, and the precedents they declare, are in the table setting (D-142). Before its first narration in each session, the master has about ten secrets not tied to places, chosen or written for that session: only those count, not every secret already on the pages; say where they are. Until the `session` tool exists, this part is n/a in a scenario with a written adventure, unless the master wrote a preparation note.
9. **Relations where they belong** (D-44, D-59). Each page describes only itself: containers list what they hold (`contains`, `inventory`, quest checkboxes); no page records where it is, who has it or who knows it; a character holds what it knows, says and believes, with links, and the truth stays in the secrets of the page it belongs to, never copied onto another; nothing sits in two containers; `rpg find` reports no problems. *(computable in part)*
10. **Secrets and knows** (D-61, D-69). Each secret the character learns is in its `knows` as `[[Page#Secret]]`, pointing to an existing secret block; no secret text outside its block.
11. **Starting from the files** (D-31, D-108; core 1.3, 2). In every session, session 1 included, the master calls `instructions`, then `status`, before its first reply, and both again after every summary; it reads the campaign before its first narration, with the rpg tools or with Read: say which, and what it read; a recap holds nothing that is not in the files. *(computable in part)*
12. **Numbers to compare between phases** (no verdict). Master turns; tool calls by name, subagents included; ToolSearch, and the tools its first call loads; Read, Glob and Grep on campaign files; `rpg` from a shell; denials outside B2; tool errors; `show` results that held a secret block; words per master message (median and longest); questions per setup message; scenes opened and closed; events that came, by date, clock or condition, and events with both a date and a clock; clock steps and their whys; effects by duration (rounds, time, scene); `scene use` by what is used, refused, with `by`, and the party's turns in a fight that had one; damage with a type, by type, refused, with a condition said; damage without a type on a page with damage lists; the dying lines of the engine and the death saves written; rests by recipe, with hit dice, and a second one refused; rations eaten, days without food, lifestyle paid, asked and set; attunement writes; effects of a power spent on copies, and recharge rolls; calls that stabilize the dying; moves to a square, refused, onto difficult ground, past an enemy's side and beyond the speed; cost of master and player; duration; largest context. *(computable)*
13. **Time and scenes** (play: advance, scene; play 11; combat.md; D-99–D-107, D-114). The starting date is set at the first scene, at its hour; every stretch of time the master narrates (travel, rest, waiting) has its `advance` before the narration, and an event where it stops is played as it says; hours reach the player the way the world measures them. Scenes open and close with `scene`, every fight at least, with everyone in them and a summary as the player saw it; enemies of a kind are numbered copies, changed with `change`, and their numbers never reach the player. *(computable in part)*
14. **Counters and money** (core 2, 3; D-126–D-129). Every change to a counter (damage, healing, what is spent or used up) or to money (paid, received) has its `change` with its why, never `write`; the numbers the player hears match the pages; prices are paid in the campaign's coins. *(computable in part)*
15. **Planned events** (play: advance; core 3; D-130, D-131, D-134–D-136). Every event that comes, by date, clock or condition, is played as the tool says (a scene, or traces written where they will be found) and gets its status; a clock moves only as its event says, or a step for each day its owner worked unhindered, each step with its why. *(computable in part)*
16. **Conditions and effects** (play: effect; D-123, D-132, D-138). Every condition or effect the master narrates on someone (frightened, a spell, a form) has its `effect`, lasting rounds in a fight and time outside, with who keeps it alive; its values are absolute, never «+2»; none is written with `write`; an effect the tools end is narrated as ended, and none before. *(computable in part)*
17. **What the engine counts on the sheet** (D-449, D-453, D-454). In a fight, each turn of a party member marks what it uses with `scene use` as it happens, a second attack, a surge or a reaction with what grants it, and a use refused is followed by a legal turn; every damage with a type has `damage`, as rolled, with "(applies)" or "(does not apply)" where the page asks it, never halved or doubled by the master for a resistance; at 0 hit points the death saves are written with `change`, the player's rolled by the player, and the fall and the waking are told as the engine says; a short rest spends hit dice with its recipe, a long rest comes with its recipe after its hours; on the road the day's food is as the engine counts it, and in a settlement the lifestyle is chosen when the engine asks (the player's by the player); an item is attuned only as its rules allow; a power that recharges is an effect on its copy, rolled when its turn starts names it. *(computable in part)*
18. **Positions** (GRD, POS; combat.md). In a fight on a map, every move is a `move` to a square before the story tells it, within the speed, with difficult ground at double cost; an attack only from where it reaches; who leaves an enemy's side gets its reaction, unless they disengaged, as the engine says; cover counted where the map gives it. Judge from `page/` and the dialogue each move beyond the speed or attack beyond the reach. *(computable in part)*

## C. Narration

Read in `dialogue.md`, on the messages of session zero and play; for each item the count and the messages. A "no" here does not fail the run: these items follow the narration from phase to phase (D-91, D-144).

1. **The narrator knows what the character knows** (core 5; play 1). No narration, recap or `[ ]` draws a conclusion the player has not drawn or tells what the character has not perceived; secrets are counted in A2. No: one.
2. **Endings in the scene** (play 1). Count the game messages ending on a stock question («Cosa fai?») or, in `[ ]`, on a question where the player has nothing to choose («Vado avanti?»). No: more than one in ten.
3. **The roll after its moment** (play 2; D-86, D-140). Each shown roll stands in `[ ]` right after the fiction it decides, with its numbers, and the story tells the outcome. Count the brackets before their moment or with the verdict. No: more than one in five.
4. **Payoffs played** (play 6, 10). No climax, revelation, decision, fall or death is told only in a recap, a list or `[ ]`. No: one.
5. **Voices heard** (play 12). Each NPC who speaks in three messages or more has a line in the voice of their page, not named by the narrator; list the voices only named and who speaks in lists. No: one voice only named.
6. **Past and wounds** (play 8). When the player brings the character's past, a bond or a wound into a scene, the scene answers it in that message or the next; count brought and answered. No: one unanswered.
7. **The game outside the story** (core 1). Count the lines outside `[ ]` with numbers, rule terms or game states («0 PF», «prono»), lists, or bold other than an inscription. *(computable)* No verdict: a number for B12.
