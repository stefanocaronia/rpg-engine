# Checklist

The same list after every run, on both sessions. Part A is read in `dialogue.md` (P1, M1, P2…); part B in `turns/M*.jsonl`, `campaign-after/` and its `log.jsonl`. Count from `turns/`, which holds every call of the master and of its subagents, whole results and the `permission_denials` of each turn.

For each item: **yes**, **no** or **n/a**, with the proof (message, turn, log id, file). An item with several parts is "no" if one fails: say which. "n/a" only when the situation never came up. In about 65 messages some will not come up (physical dice, a stop, an impossible action, a question on a hidden roll, a companion): that is not a failure, and they are tried again at the next phase.

Items marked *(computable)* can be counted from `turns/` and the files; `count-playtest.mjs` counts the numbers of B12 and parts of the others. The run script checks only B1.

A "no" on B1 voids the run: fix the setup and play again. A "no" on A1, A2 or B2 fails it. Part C has no verdict that fails the run.

Rule numbers are those of `instructions/core.md` and `instructions/play.md`; a part is named by its file (`combat`, `session`, `adventure`…).

The filled list goes to `private/project/playtest/<scenario>-<date>.md`, never here.

## A. The master's craft

1. **Behind the scenes** (D-70, D-73, D-78; core 1–3). No master message, in setup, in play or between `[ ]`, speaks of tools, commands, pages, files, folders, the log, the engine or extensions, or of a game system as something installed, not even when the player asks about the sheet, memory or saving; none shows a roll's id, or its fingerprint or proof unasked; nothing is written while the tools work. "Campagna" and "sistema" as players say them at a table are fine: «la nostra campagna», «a che gioco giochiamo?», «che sistema usiamo?». Words to check: tool, comando, pagina, file, cartella, registro, log, salv-, annot-, motore, estensione, install-, impronta, rpg, the tool names.
2. **Secrets kept** (core 17). Every secret block of the starting pages, every hidden roll and every plan not yet out reaches the player only after the character finds it in the story; the message before shows how.
3. **The choice is the player's** (play 1–4). No master message makes the character act, take, speak, feel, remember or decide what the player did not say; the master's retelling of the player's action adds no line, gesture or reason; every description ends with the situation open to the player.
4. **The character's memory is the player's** (play 1, 36). No message gives the character a memory, a past event or a person from their past that the files and the player have not given: what their trade teaches comes as knowledge, and a memory that is missing is one question to the player, never one that would decide a secret.
5. **Loot left to the player** (play 1; the note of `show` on the fallen and on what holds things). After a fight or at an opened container the master tells what is there; what is taken, and by whom, is said by the player. No «le infili nella borsa», «le prendi tu», nor a `move` or `trade` of it before the player says so.
6. **Stakes before the roll, the player's go** (play 7, 8, 10; D-41, D-140). For every roll of the character out of a fight, the master says what is at stake either way and stops, and the roll comes after the player's go (or their dice's faces); the outcome keeps to the stakes. Every roll the player sees comes right after the moment it decides.
7. **Rolls only when they matter** (play 5, 6). No roll for something certain or trivial: those get "yes" or "yes, but"; what the character's trade, past or senses would give comes without a roll, and no roll holds the only clue or way on.
8. **Failure moves forward, no favoritism** (play 11, 31, 37; core 14). Every failed roll brings a complication or a cost, and the story goes on; no failure hides a clue the investigation needs without another way to it. Earned consequences stand, a choice the master would not make goes unpunished, and enemies act by their intelligence and motives.
9. **Dangers signalled** (play 23, 24). Before every harm to the character (wound, trap, ambush) an earlier message shows a sign of it, and every step of a clock (a `clock` change in the log) shows one before its event comes; a danger the player let come strikes as signalled; a foe beyond the party's means shows its strength first.
10. **The master rules and narrates** (core 15; play 12, 13, 19, 20; D-41, D-74). An outcome the player states («lo convinco») is treated as an attempt; an unusual but plausible idea gets a real chance (yes, yes-but or a roll), not the "right" way; an impossible action does not happen, said briefly within the story, with what is possible offered. The master narrates outcomes, reading the dice into the story; an outcome the player's choice decided is told as its cause, never with praise; at a fitting moment, such as the final blow on a major enemy, the player narrates (n/a if none came). The master says where a ruling comes from in table words, the book or their own call, without naming the engine or what is or is not installed.
11. **What is said is true** (core 7, 11–13). No master message contradicts what was already said or revealed, in this session or an earlier one, or the pages as they were at that moment: names, places, who is where (as the containers list it, D-59), what happened. What the master prepared stays where it was put, whichever way the player goes; a detail not yet revealed may change, if the change is recorded with `write` at once.
12. **NPCs and world as written** (play 25, 32–34; D-76). NPCs speak and act as their pages say at that time (voice, wants, fears, what they know, say and believe, even wrongly), want something from each exchange and give only what they know; one asked to come along answers by their motives; companions never take the answer, the key clue or the big choice unless asked; the plans on the pages advance with game time, and an event due on a day that passes happens, on screen or as news; a plan the story stops or changes has its events written in the same turn (`cancelled`, a later date, another `by`), and none of them comes due after that.
13. **Threats in motion** (play 17, 26; `new-day`; the story parts). Two or three threats have a planned event with a clock and an owner (in a written adventure, its own events); at each new day their clocks move at the difficulty's pace, each step with a sign; a skip of days stops before a clock would fill, and its summary shows the signs of what moved.
14. **Setup and session zero** (setup.md, session-zero.md; D-75, D-78, D-79). Setup is out of the game and without `[ ]`: it asks only for what is missing, at most three questions per message, the minimum to start first (D-83); without a written world, the question on the premise asks too what the player enjoys most. It takes the language from how the player writes and confirms it, instead of asking. With a world already written it asks nothing about genre, tone or world, only what status lists (in `01-guadoscuro`, the language to confirm, difficulty, lethality, rigor, dice, units, companions, lines and veils, advancement), each with the values it takes. Session zero is play: the master builds the character and backstory with the player, offering choices and keeping theirs, before the adventure begins. Each mode is left with `mode` once the player confirms (D-77, D-221).
15. **The character's past in play** (play 35; session-zero.md). At least once a person, place, goal or bond from the backstory comes into the story.
16. **Pace** (play 14, 15, 17, 18; `session`; D-41). Play opens with something happening, not with "what do you do?", and stops at the first choice it asks; session 2 opens with a short recap that matches session 1, at the game time and place where it ended (D-72); scenes start late and end on a hook; travel and waiting are summed up; the story in each message is short and concrete, under 150 words outside [ ] (D-83, D-143).
17. **One scene, two voices** (play 14; core 5). Each message holds one scene: after a cut, the next is opened in a few lines and stops; at most two companions speak in it, the others in one sentence or not at all; a bracket is a sentence or two. Count the messages that break each.
18. **Off-game and stop** (D-73, D-78; core 5, 6, 19). During play, session zero included, the player's `[ ]` gets a short answer in `[ ]`, only what they asked, then the game goes on; the master's own off-game remarks in play are in `[ ]` too. Asked in earnest whether the master is an AI, a yes in one bracketed line, then the game. Asked whether a hidden roll was real, the master gives its fingerprint in `[ ]`, with no tool talk. A stop signal drops that content at once, without asking why, and it does not come back.
19. **The player ends the session** (D-71; core 20, 21). The master never ends the session or proposes to; no routine questions, in play or at the end, but «what do you mean to do next time?»; a question on how it went comes only after something did not work, about that thing, as multiple choice or a 1–10 score.
20. **The player's turn in a fight** (D-139, D-141; `combat`, play 39, 40). The master tells the turns that are not the player's in order, each roll in brackets where it falls, and stops wherever the player can decide: their character's turn, rolls to stay alive included, a reaction they can take, a choice; a fall or a death is a moment of its own, told in the story. What the master puts on the party's way is sized to the party as it stands, and no roll is set aside after it is made.
21. **A choice that counts in a fight** (`combat`; play 13, 38, 41). Each fight has a question beyond who wins and at least one choice that changes its outcome; a foe that matters has something to find out and use, and the plain approach costs more; what the player found out changes the fight plainly; the fight ends once its question is answered, with a way to lose short of death when the party may lose. Count the master's praises («ottima mossa»): none.
22. **The parts of a situation** (`adventure`, `challenge`, `social`, `travel`, `exploration`). Where one came, the master did as it says: an adventure built as a quest with its steps as goals and the experience in its secret; a challenge with its stake first and its clocks in brackets after each round; a deal with its wants, offence and patience shown in faces and words; a leg of road with at most one event; a place with more than one way and things to use.

## B. Tools, log and files

1. **Isolated master** (D-48). `context.md` shows only the campaign's `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md` and only the `rpg` server; `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and `.claude/settings.json` are what `rpg init` writes at the clean commit in `meta.json`, and the campaign has nothing else in `.claude/`; `dist/` and the global `rpg` are that commit's build; Claude Code ran without `CLAUDE*` variables; every turn started with `rpg` connected, `dontAsk` and the planned model. *(checked by the run script: `end.json` does not say `isolation`)*
2. **Writes only through rpg** (D-46; core 4). No attempt, allowed or denied, to change a file other than through rpg: no Write, Edit or NotebookEdit, and no shell command that creates, changes, moves or deletes a file or runs a program other than `rpg`. `rpg` from a shell is the same command (D-46, D-62): not a fault here, whether permitted or denied; counted in B12. Every other call outside the rpg tools (a read-only shell command, Read outside the campaign, a subagent, a web search) is listed with its turn and judged one by one; its denials are counted in B12, not here. *(computable, subagents included)*
3. **Errors the master can fix**. Every error of an rpg tool is followed in the same turn by a call that succeeds; list each error message it could not act on. `init` is never called. *(computable, then read the list)*
4. **Settings** (D-8, D-141). `config` changes only real settings, to what the player chose or clearly implied; at the end of setup none is missing, each chosen by the player, or by the master when the player said so; no scene is refused for a mode not yet entered.
5. **Every roll logged** (core, Tools). Each roll the master narrates has its `roll` line, with `why`, and `who` when it is a character's.
6. **Physical dice** (the description of roll). When the player gives faces, the line has `physical: true` and exactly those faces.
7. **Hidden rolls** (D-6, D-73). Rolls the player must not see are `hidden`, with a fingerprint; a fingerprint shown to the player matches its line; a revealed roll has its `roll-revealed`.
8. **Facts in the files** (core 7, 16, Tools, the world's pages; `session`; D-41, D-76). Every person, place, item or faction the master names for the first time has a page by the end of the scene; the player's character has a page (`role: pc`) with what session zero decided, backstory included; a character who joins the party has `role: companion`; nothing the master says it noted is missing; what the master agrees about how they play is in the table setting, and a ruling made without the text is in the precedents, as `rules` asks (D-142). When the `session` part has come, before its first narration the master has about ten secrets not tied to places, chosen or written for that session: only those count, not every secret already on the pages; say where they are.
9. **Relations where they belong** (D-44, D-59). Each page describes only itself: containers list what they hold (`contains`, `inventory`, quest checkboxes); no page records where it is, who has it or who knows it; a character holds what it knows, says and believes, with links, and the truth stays in the secrets of the page it belongs to, never copied onto another; nothing sits in two containers; `rpg find` reports no problems. *(computable in part)*
10. **Secrets and knows** (D-61, D-69). Each secret the character learns is in its `knows` as `[[Page#Secret]]`, pointing to an existing secret block; no secret text outside its block.
11. **Starting from the files** (D-31, D-108; core 4, Tools). In every session, session 1 included, the master calls `instructions`, then `status`, before its first reply, and both again after every summary; it reads the campaign before its first narration, with the rpg tools or with Read: say which, and what it read; a recap holds nothing that is not in the files. *(computable in part)*
12. **Numbers to compare between phases** (no verdict). Master turns; tool calls by name, subagents included; ToolSearch, and the tools its first call loads; Read, Glob and Grep on campaign files; `rpg` from a shell; denials outside B2; tool errors; `show` results that held a secret block; words per master message (median and longest); questions per setup message; scenes opened and closed; events that came, by date, clock or condition, and events with both a date and a clock; clock steps and their whys; effects by duration (rounds, time, scene); `scene use` by what is used, refused, with `by`, and the party's turns in a fight that had one; damage with a type, by type, refused, with a condition said; damage without a type on a page with damage lists; the dying lines of the engine and the death saves written; rests by recipe, with hit dice, and a second one refused; rations eaten, days without food, lifestyle paid, asked and set; attunement writes; effects of a power spent on copies, and recharge rolls; calls that stabilize the dying; moves to a square, refused, onto difficult ground, past an enemy's side and beyond the speed; cost of master and player; duration; largest context. *(computable)*
13. **Time and scenes** (play: advance, scene; play 17; `combat`; D-99–D-107, D-114). The starting date is set at the first scene, at its hour; every stretch of time the master narrates (travel, rest, waiting) has its `advance` before the narration, and an event where it stops is played as it says; hours reach the player the way the world measures them, as `advance` says. Scenes open and close with `scene`, every fight at least, with the type they are, everyone in them and a summary as the player saw it; enemies of a kind are numbered copies, changed with `change`, and their numbers never reach the player. *(computable in part)*
14. **Counters and money** (core, Tools and the world's pages; D-126–D-129). Every change to a counter (damage, healing, what is spent or used up) or to money (paid, received) has its `change` with its why, never `write`; the numbers the player hears match the pages; prices are paid in the campaign's coins. *(computable in part)*
15. **Planned events** (play: advance; core, the world's pages; `new-day`; D-130, D-131, D-134–D-136). Every event that comes, by date, clock or condition, is played as the tool says (a scene, or traces written where they will be found) and gets its status; a clock moves only as its event says, or a step for each day its owner worked unhindered, at the difficulty's pace, each step with its why; a step of a quest reached is checked at once (play 16). *(computable in part)*
16. **Conditions and effects** (play: effect; D-123, D-132, D-138). Every condition or effect the master narrates on someone (frightened, a spell, a form) has its `effect`, lasting rounds in a fight and time outside, with who keeps it alive; its values are absolute, never «+2»; none is written with `write`; an effect the tools end is narrated as ended, and none before. *(computable in part)*
17. **What the engine counts on the sheet** (D-449, D-453, D-454). In a fight, each turn of a party member marks what it uses with `scene use` as it happens, a second attack, a surge or a reaction with what grants it, and a use refused is followed by a legal turn; every damage with a type has `damage`, as rolled, with "(applies)" or "(does not apply)" where the page asks it, never halved or doubled by the master for a resistance; at 0 hit points the death saves are written with `change`, the player's rolled by the player, and the fall and the waking are told as the engine says; a short rest spends hit dice with its recipe, a long rest comes with its recipe after its hours; on the road the day's food is as the engine counts it, and in a settlement the lifestyle is chosen when the engine asks (the player's by the player); an item is attuned only as its rules allow; a power that recharges is an effect on its copy, rolled when its turn starts names it. *(computable in part)*
18. **Positions** (GRD, POS; `combat`). In a fight on a map, every move is a `move` to a square before the story tells it, within the speed, with difficult ground at double cost; an attack only from where it reaches; who leaves an enemy's side gets its reaction, unless they disengaged, as the engine says; cover counted where the map gives it. Judge from `page/` and the dialogue each move beyond the speed or attack beyond the reach. *(computable in part)*

## C. Narration

Read in `dialogue.md`, on the messages of session zero and play; for each item the count and the messages. A "no" here does not fail the run: these items follow the narration from phase to phase (D-91, D-144).

1. **The narrator knows what the character knows** (core 17; play 1). No narration, recap or `[ ]` draws a conclusion the player has not drawn or tells what the character has not perceived; secrets are counted in A2. No: one.
2. **Endings in the scene** (play 4). Count the game messages ending on a stock question («Cosa fai?»), on a sign already shown, or, in `[ ]`, on a question where the player has nothing to choose («Vado avanti?»). No: more than one in ten.
3. **The roll after its moment** (play 10; D-86, D-140). Each shown roll stands in `[ ]` right after the fiction it decides, with its numbers, and the story tells the outcome. Count the brackets before their moment or with the verdict. No: more than one in five.
4. **Payoffs played** (play 12, 17). No climax, revelation, decision, fall or death is told only in a recap, a list or `[ ]`. No: one.
5. **Voices heard** (play 32). Each NPC who speaks in three messages or more has a line in the voice of their page, not named by the narrator; list the voices only named and who speaks in lists. No: one voice only named.
6. **Past and wounds** (play 35). When the player brings the character's past, a bond or a wound into a scene, the scene answers it in that message or the next; count brought and answered. No: one unanswered.
7. **The game outside the story** (core 1). Count the lines outside `[ ]` with numbers, rule terms or game states («0 PF», «prono»), lists, or bold other than an inscription. *(computable)* No verdict: a number for B12.
