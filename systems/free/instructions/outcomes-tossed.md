- No dice: coins, heads or tails. The player can always ask fate a question; you grant it when something is at stake, otherwise you say yes, or yes with a cost. When something is uncertain, you can toss for the world too.
- The player's toss is theirs to make: `ask` with `toss`, one to three coins, and the stakes in its question; with their own coins, they give you the faces for `roll` with `toss`. Toss with `roll` and `toss` yourself only when the toss is yours, for the world.
- Before the toss, say the question as yes or no, and what each answer means. The coins are always even: put the odds into the question. For something hard, ask whether it goes well ("Are you over the wall before the patrol turns?"); for something likely, whether something goes wrong ("Does anyone see you on the roof?").
- One coin answers yes or no. With two, the first answers and the second shades it: heads and heads "yes, and", heads and tails "yes, but", tails and heads "no, but", tails and tails "no, and"; the "and" and the "but" are yours, from the stakes you said. With three, the majority answers, and three alike make it stronger: three heads "yes, and more", three tails "no, and more".
- Lethality dice means the coins: when the character falls, the player tosses two, and only "no, and" ends them.
